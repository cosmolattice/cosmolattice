Dear reader, here you can find all the details on the theoretical aspects behind $\mathcal{C}\mathtt{osmo}\mathcal{L}\mathtt{attice}$. 

In [Lattice Field Theory Primer](Brief_Review_On_Lattice_Techniques.md), we first introduce the basic notions of **Lattice Field Theory**, including the characterization of a lattice itself as a discrete set of spatial sites, the introduction of finite-difference operators on a lattice reproducing differential operations of the continuum --- including gauge-invariant lattice field-theory techniques ---, the definition of the lattice-momentum associated to finite-difference operators, and the characterization of fields' power spectra on a lattice. We then discuss briefly about the initial condition and evolution algorithms required to solve partial differential equations (PDE's) represented by finite difference equations on a lattice. All these techniques, when put together to solve problems characterized by a set of PDE's that describe the field dynamics of early Universe scenarios ($e.g.$ inflation, preheating, phase transitions, topological defects, etc), are collectively referred to as **Lattice Cosmology Techniques (LCT)**, or simply **Lattice Cosmology (LC)**.
 
-  Section [Monographic Reviews](MonographicReviews.md) presents the theoretical basis for the equations implemented in $\mathcal{C}\mathtt{osmo}\mathcal{L}\mathtt{attice}$. As the theoretical framework - based on LC Techniques - is quite broad, we preset the material split in a series of monographic reviews on latttice techniques downloadable in PDF format, *The art of simulating the early Universe*, *Part I* (Ref. [@Figueroa_2020rrl]), *Part II* (Ref. [@BaezaBallesteros_2025tme]), and *Part III* (Ref. [@Figueroa_2026XYZ]). These reviews, which are referred to, colloquially, as $\mathtt{The~Art-I}$, $\mathtt{The~Art-II}$ and $\mathtt{The~Art-III}$ monographs, provide comprehensive discussions on the lattice methods used in $\mathcal{C}\mathtt{osmo}\mathcal{L}\mathtt{attice}$ for scalar–gauge field theories in an expanding universe ($\mathtt{The~Art-I}$[@Figueroa_2020rrl]), gravitational waves and non-canonical aspects of field theory - including interactions, initial conditions, dimensionality - ($\mathtt{The~Art-II}$ [@BaezaBallesteros_2025tme]), and fluid dynamics as a description of an ensemble of interacting scalar, gauge, and fermion fields ($\mathtt{The~Art-III}$[@Figueroa_2026XYZ]).
